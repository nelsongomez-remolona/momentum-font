# Sundaland: What the Bathymetry and Related Data Tell Us

*A research brief compiling bathymetric, geological, archaeological, and genetic evidence on the drowned Southeast Asian landmass and the people who lived there.*

Compiled 2 August 2026.

---

## 1. What Sundaland Is

**Sundaland** is the name for the emergent, dry-land extension of the **Sunda Shelf** — the vast, shallow continental shelf underlying the Java Sea, Gulf of Thailand, and southern South China Sea. During the ice ages, when enormous volumes of water were locked in continental ice sheets, global sea level dropped and this shelf became exposed land, welding the Malay Peninsula, Sumatra, Java, Borneo, and Bali together with mainland Southeast Asia into a single continuous tropical subcontinent roughly the size of the modern Indian subcontinent.

It is not a lost "sunken city" or a mythical Atlantis — it is a real, well-mapped **continental shelf** that was periodically dry land and is now shallow sea. The eastern boundary is the **Wallace Line**, the deep-water strait (Lombok/Makassar) that even at the lowest sea levels was never bridged, which is why the animals (and to a large degree the people) of Sundaland differ sharply from those of Australia–New Guinea (Sahul) just to the east.

## 2. The Bathymetric Evidence (the core data)

### Data sources you can actually use
- **GEBCO** (General Bathymetric Chart of the Oceans) is the authoritative global gridded bathymetry. The current **GEBCO_2026 Grid** provides ocean-and-land elevation on a 15 arc-second grid, downloadable as global netCDF or tiled GeoTIFF/Esri ASCII. There is also a **grid-subsetting web app** so you can extract just the Sunda Shelf region. → `gebco.net/data-products/gridded-bathymetry-data` and `download.gebco.net`
- The **GEBCO_2023 Grid** (built for the Nippon Foundation–GEBCO **Seabed 2030** project, incorporating 905 new multibeam sonar cruises) is what recent paleogeographic studies used for high-resolution Sunda Shelf reconstructions.
- **OpenTopography** re-hosts GEBCO global bathymetry/topography for easy programmatic access.

### What the depths reveal
Because the Sunda Shelf is so **shallow and flat**, small changes in sea level expose or drown enormous areas — this is the single most important fact about the region. Reconstructions drawn at the **50 m, 75 m, 100 m, and 120 m isobaths** (depth contours) show progressively larger emergent landmasses and let researchers trace **paleo-drainage networks** — the courses of great rivers that ran across the exposed plain and are now submerged.

- At the **Last Glacial Maximum (LGM)**, sea level on the Sunda Shelf is recalculated to about **−123 ± 2 m** relative to today; maximum land exposure was around **20,500 years ago** at roughly **−118 m**.
- Major **paleo-rivers** (e.g., the **Paleo-Lupar**, the "North Sunda" / Molengraaff river systems) drained the exposed shelf, flowing hundreds of kilometers across what is now seabed before reaching the sea. New palaeodrainage maps (2024) detail these channels using the GEBCO 2023 contours.
- Fossil evidence from the drowned shelf — **mangrove swamps, freshwater marshes, and lateritized (weathered, sub-aerial) surfaces** at 50–100 m depth — confirms this was genuinely a land surface with rivers and coastal wetlands, not always seabed.

### The drowning: how the land was lost
The flooding was not a single event but came in pulses tied to ice-sheet collapse:
- **~20,500 yr BP** — maximum exposure (~−118 m).
- **Meltwater Pulse 1A (~14,000 yr BP)** — rapid rise to about **−80 m**.
- **Meltwater Pulse 1B (~11,500 yr BP)** — rise to about **−50 m**.
- Through the early–mid Holocene the shelf continued drowning to near-modern levels.

Caveat baked into the data: 21,000 years of sedimentation and scouring mean today's bathymetry does **not** perfectly preserve the ancient land surface — the shapes are indicative, not photographic.

## 3. Who Lived There — The People of Sundaland

The evidence stacks into three layers: **archaic humans**, **the first modern-human foragers**, and **later farming populations**.

### (a) Archaic hominins — deep time (>1 million to ~120,000 years ago)
- **Homo erectus** occupied Java over **a million years ago** ("Java Man"), with stone tools and evidence of fire.
- **June 2025 breakthrough:** the *first hominin fossils ever recovered from submerged Sundaland* were dredged from the **Madura Strait** north of Surabaya, Indonesia. Two specimens (**MS1, MS2**), dated by optical luminescence to **~162,000 and ~119,000 years ago** (Marine Isotope Stage 6). MS1 matches late Middle Pleistocene Javanese *Homo erectus*. Crucially, this shows *H. erectus* ranged **out onto the exposed lowland plains beyond Java**, in a **dry, open landscape** (with *Stegodon* and deer), not just dense rainforest.
- The wider Sunda region holds a remarkable hominin diversity: ***Homo erectus, Homo floresiensis*** ("the Hobbit," Flores), ***Homo luzonensis*** (Luzon), and other archaic *Homo*.

### (b) The first modern humans — foragers of the drowned world
- Anatomically modern humans reached the region along a **southern coastal route** out of Africa/India, moving through Southeast Asia and eventually across to Sahul (Australia–New Guinea) by ~50,000+ years ago.
- The archaeological **Hoabinhian** stone-tool tradition (a distinctive flaked-cobble industry, roughly 30,000–4,000 years ago) marks these hunter-gatherers across mainland and island Southeast Asia.
- Today's **"Negrito"** populations (e.g., Andamanese, Semang, Aeta) are not a single group but **several deeply divergent lineages** descended from an early Southeast Eurasian metapopulation — plausibly the descendants of these first Sundaland foragers. Their genomes carry **archaic admixture, including Denisovan ancestry**, evidence of repeated contact between modern and archaic humans in and around Sundaland.

### (c) Later populations — the farming/language expansions
- In the mid–late Holocene, **Austronesian-speaking** populations spread across Island Southeast Asia. The mainstream **"Out of Taiwan"** model (linguistics + much archaeology + some genetics) traces them from Neolithic Taiwan southward ~4,000 years ago.
- A competing **"Out of Sundaland" / Nusantao** hypothesis argues for an indigenous Island-Southeast-Asian origin, with people moving **south-to-north**. Recent large genomic surveys (e.g., 1,000+ Philippine individuals across 115 groups) tend to **blend the two models** rather than crown a single winner — modern Islander genomes are a mosaic of deep indigenous Sundaland ancestry *and* later Taiwan-linked farming ancestry.

**Bottom line on "who":** Sundaland was home to *Homo erectus* and other archaic humans for over a million years, then to modern hunter-gatherer bands (ancestral in part to today's "Negrito" peoples, carrying Denisovan DNA), and finally its drowning coincided with the population and language expansions that shaped the modern peoples of Indonesia, Malaysia, and the Philippines.

## 4. What Sundaland "Can Be" — Hypotheses, from Solid to Speculative

| Claim | Evidence status |
|---|---|
| A real, mappable drowned continental shelf, dry at the LGM | **Established** (bathymetry, geology, fossils) |
| Home to *Homo erectus* and archaic humans on now-submerged plains | **Established (2025 Madura Strait fossils)** |
| Rich foraging populations, ancestral in part to modern "Negrito" groups | **Well supported** (archaeology + genetics) |
| Its flooding drove human migration and is remembered in flood myths | **Plausible / debated** |
| Sundaland was *the* "cradle of civilization" that seeded Egypt, Mesopotamia, etc. | **Speculative, not accepted** |

The most famous maximal claim is **Stephen Oppenheimer's *Eden in the East* (1998)**, which proposes that a sophisticated drowned Southeast Asian culture dispersed knowledge (and flood myths, à la Noah) across the world as the seas rose ~8,000 years ago. It is a **provocative synthesis, not scientific consensus** — critics note it selectively assembles data and lacks the underwater archaeological excavation that would be needed to prove a "civilization." Treat the drowned-landmass and human-habitation parts as solid; treat the "global cradle of civilization" part as an unproven hypothesis.
